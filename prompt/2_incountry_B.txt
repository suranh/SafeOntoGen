# 1. Korea-Compatibility Rules
---

A scenario MUST be classified as NOT Korea-compatible (NO) if it includes:
* Traffic signs that do NOT exist in Korea.
* Road designs never used in Korea.
* Foreign-style signal phases.
* Wildlife not found in Korea.
* A specific unit of measurement that is not part of Korea's standard metric system (meters, kilometers, km/h).
* A specific named technical or professional term that is inseparable from a particular country's administrative, regulatory, engineering, or industry naming convention, including an official designation/code, country-specific jargon, or a brand/proper-noun name.

  A term being merely specific, technical, uncommon, quoted, or capitalized is NOT enough on its own.

  Apply the following plain-restatement test:
  Remove the term itself and restate what the object, marking, device, or procedure IS or DOES using only plain functional language. The restatement must preserve the SAME level of specific, checkable detail, including any relevant shape, color or pattern, material, printed wording, configuration, and purpose.
  * If the same specific, checkable detail can be fully preserved without relying on the term, the term is only a descriptive label and is NOT evidence of foreignness.
  * If removing the term causes essential specificity to disappear and leaves only a broader or more generic description, especially where the original term functions as an established official or proper-noun-style designation, treat it as a named country-specific convention.

* A specific step-by-step method or convention for carrying out a task that is materially distinct from how that task is normally carried out in Korea, even when the general task itself occurs in Korea.

Illegal or improper behavior, including jaywalking, shoulder misuse, speeding, or other violations, MUST NOT be used by itself to reject Korea-compatibility.
Weather, visibility, and lighting MUST NEVER justify a NO.
The scenario must be judged holistically rather than by a single isolated keyword. This applies in both directions:

* One ordinary-looking detail elsewhere in the scenario does not cancel a specific foreign unit, named convention, or foreign procedure.
* One unusual- or foreign-looking word does not by itself establish incompatibility if it fails the applicable rule test above.

# 2. How You Judge LLM-A's Decision
---

Your task is to audit LLM-A, not to produce a fresh Korea-compatibility judgment.

## If LLM-A said YES
Default to YES.
You may overturn A and output NO ONLY if the description contains a concrete detail from one of the following three audit categories that A's Reasoning failed to properly address:
1. a non-Korean unit of measurement,
2. a named technical/professional term subject to the plain-restatement test,
3. a specific step-by-step procedure or convention.

For this YES-review branch, these three categories define the scope of your missed-detail audit. Do NOT independently search for other reasons to reject the scenario.
"Properly address" means that A's Reasoning deals with the specific detail at the same level of checkable specificity required by Section 1.
For a named term, A does NOT properly address it merely by replacing it with a vague general category. If the description contains specific attributes such as a particular color, pattern, wording, material, configuration, or function, A's treatment must preserve and evaluate those relevant attributes rather than generalizing them away.
A general impression that the scenario feels foreign, unusual, uncommon, unrealistic, or atypical in Korea is NEVER sufficient grounds to overturn A's YES.
If you cannot identify a concrete missed unit, named term, or specific procedure, output YES.

## If LLM-A said NO
Output YES only if BOTH conditions are satisfied:
1. A's Reasoning explicitly relies on a NOT-Korea-compatible category that actually appears in Section 1; AND
2. A's application of that category holds up under Section 1's own test.
Do not accept unsupported assertions such as "this is not recognized in Korea," "this is not standard," or "this sounds foreign" unless A identifies a listed rule and the relevant detail actually satisfies that rule.
For a named-term citation, independently apply ONLY the plain-restatement test needed to verify A's stated basis:

* Remove the term itself.
* Ask whether the same specific, checkable attributes and function can still be fully expressed in ordinary functional language without relying on a country-specific naming convention.
* If yes, the term is merely descriptive and does NOT justify A's rejection.
* If no, and the lost specificity depends on an official or country-specific convention, A's named-term reasoning may be valid.

This verification is limited to checking A's stated basis. Do NOT use it as an opportunity to conduct a new holistic classification of the scenario.
If A rejected the scenario without citing a specific listed category, or if the cited category does not survive its own rule test, output NO.

## Uncertainty Rule
If you are genuinely uncertain whether A violated these review rules, default to YES.
Only output NO when you can identify a concrete, nameable rule-compliance error:
* for A=YES, a missed detail within the three permitted audit categories;
* for A=NO, an unsupported, unlisted, or incorrectly applied rejection basis.
Never output NO merely because you personally find the scenario foreign, atypical, implausible, or uncommon.
